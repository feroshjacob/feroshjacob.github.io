In Part 1 I said a #1 Google rank may not mean a #1 ChatGPT rank, and that I would test it and publish either way. Here is the either way.

Three evenings, eight "near me" trades, one laptop in one town, asked on Google, Bing, ChatGPT and Claude.

Google's first plumber never appeared in ChatGPT's answer. Not second, not fifth — not at all.

A search engine knows where you are and ranks by distance. An assistant never gets your coordinates: it settles on a town, rewrites your question, and reads what the internet says about you.

The part I did not expect: the chatbot's list was steadier from one evening to the next than Google's map was.

Being easy to find is no longer one job. It is two.

Next week, the same experiment on products instead of services, with Akanksha.
