Coding is getting cheap. That doesn't mean the developer's job disappears — it means it moves.

Part 6 of this series lays out three specific, checkable claims about the next five years: developers become conductors running several AI agents in parallel, software's purchasing power explodes because custom work finally gets cheap enough to be worth building, and the whole thing is gated by something unglamorous — getting an agent enough real context about one specific, messy business without it confidently building the wrong thing.

The part I didn't expect going in: two of those three hypotheses are conditional on the third. Conducting agents instead of writing code, and buying more software for the same dollar, only pay off if the context problem gets solved first.

I kept the METR counterexample in this one too, for the same reason it mattered in the last article — it's the finding that keeps this series an investigation instead of a pitch, and here it does new work, showing up as the reason the "conductor" role isn't automatically a good deal.
