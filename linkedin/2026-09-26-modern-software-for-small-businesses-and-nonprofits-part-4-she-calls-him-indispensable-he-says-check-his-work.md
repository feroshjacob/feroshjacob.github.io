Terri Hitzig calls ChatGPT "Chatty Chat Chat" and refers to it as "he."

I asked her ten questions about how she actually uses it, and asked her own ChatGPT the same ten, separately. Neither saw the other's answers. The interesting part was not where they agreed.

Terri's answer to "where should you not rely on it" was one line: "Always rely on Chat." Its answer ran several paragraphs listing the decisions that should stay hers — how a client is behaving, whether the food tastes right, whether a price is sustainable after doing the labor — and ended with this: "Terri should treat confidence from me with suspicion."

Asked for a time it got something wrong, she remembered small misses. It volunteered a bigger one against itself: it had read ambiguous handwriting and named possible drug names far too confidently, where a wrong reference could change what gets communicated to a carrier. She caught it, because she checks questionable readings instead of copying them through.

She pays $20 a month and says he is worth it. It says she gets poor value every time it answers confidently something that should have been verified.

A tool arguing for its own limits while its user argues for its indispensability is a more honest story than another productivity review — and a useful one if you run a business on your own.
