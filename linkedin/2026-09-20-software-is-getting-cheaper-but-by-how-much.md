One of the first things you learn working with AI: ask for evidence.

I've spent months arguing AI is making software cheaper. So I applied that rule to my own claim — and went looking for the actual research instead of a vibe.

The honest answer: there's no single "software is X% cheaper" number. Developer productivity, labor cost, production cost, market price, and what I call software purchasing power can all move in different directions at once. A Microsoft Research study of 4,867 developers found a 26% productivity gain. McKinsey found 32% of companies are now building what they used to buy. But METR's study of experienced developers on real codebases found the opposite — 19% slower, even though they believed AI had helped.

The more useful question isn't "how much cheaper is software?" It's "how much more software can I buy for $1,000 today?" Same money, more functionality — that's the pattern the evidence actually supports.
