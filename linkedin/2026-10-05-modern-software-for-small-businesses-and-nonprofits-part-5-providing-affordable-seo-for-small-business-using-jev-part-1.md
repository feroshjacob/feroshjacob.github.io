SEO tools that manage more than five or six sites at once can easily cost $1,000 a month. An agency reselling that has to recover it across clients — which is several thousand dollars a month landing on one small business. That's not a budget decision; it's just out of reach.

So I'm building a small, semi-automated tool that enters one approved business record once and prepares it for ten listing platforms (Foursquare, Bing, Yelp, Nextdoor, BBB, and others) — with Jev, a narrow AI decision model, helping only where a page or category is genuinely ambiguous.

"Semi-automated" is not a hedge. Each platform documents something different about what automation it actually permits, and the app defaults every undocumented permission to deny. Only one of the ten — Foursquare — has a documented grant for automation via its official API. Angi is blocked outright: its signup funnel authorizes marketing calls and texts, and no free, no-lead route is documented.

Jev never drives a browser, fills a field, or clicks submit. It picks among a closed set of candidates from compact page state, and its answer always comes back non-executable — a human reviews every choice before it becomes a published fact.

The honest build log is in the post: a browser-level privacy bug that unit tests alone would have missed, and four separate rounds of adversarial review that rejected an already-green test suite before release.

The project is still in progress — ten sites, one platform fully adapter-automated so far. Open-sourcing it if it works.
